⚡ Power & E-Mode — Generators, Poles & the Planner's Call

You don't have to decide about power any more — the planner decides, per plan, and says so in plain words: whether a grid earns its place, exactly which machines to switch to E-Mode, how many generators and poles that takes (usually far fewer than you own), and how many Aniimo better off you end up. The call rebuilds with every change, like everything else here.

🔌 What the pieces actually do

Crackle Generator (2 × 2, covers 11 × 11) makes the grid's electricity — but it doesn't run itself: one Lightning Aniimo works each generator, and its supply is capped by its level, which follows your Power Module: Lv 1 = 600, Lv 2 = 800, Lv 3 = 1,000, Lv 4 = 1,200, Lv 5 = 1,500 (Lv 1 at RV 12, then a level every two RVs — Lv 4 at RV 18). A strong Lightning Aniimo gets that full cap out of it.

Crackle Power Pole (1.5 × 1.5, covers 7 × 7) makes no power of its own. It is a relay: it stretches the grid's reach, and it only passes power on when it overlaps an area that is already powered. A facility touching any powered area gets electricity.

E-Mode (from RV 12): a processing facility inside the grid can run on a timer instead of an Aniimo — that Aniimo is freed for other work or for home. Every switched-on facility draws power: 15 × its level for most, 30 × level for the Mine, Well, Aniipod Maker and Dance Pad Polisher. While supply beats demand, the grid runs faster — up to 120%; the moment demand passes supply, everything on the grid slows below 100%.

🧭 How the planner makes the call

It reads the machines your current plan actually runs — basket, strategy and Step 5 additions together — and tries the smallest grid first:

  • Cheapest machines first. Copies switch on in order of power draw, lowest first, under the fewest generators that hold them — and never past the supply, so a recommended grid can never slow anything down.
  • A grid must pay for its Aniimo. Each generator out costs a Lightning Aniimo who stays at that post. The call only says yes when the machines freed outnumber the generators by at least 2 — otherwise the honest answer is no grid, and your Aniimo stay on their machines (where most of them also haul their own output to storage; a generator Aniimo never does).
  • Never switched on: Mines, Wells, Aniipod Makers and Dance Pad Polishers — at 30 × level, one can eat a whole generator. The gather posts (Sandcastle, Dewy House, Nimbus Bed, Hammock, Floral Windmill) keep their Aniimo because their family gates need one standing there, and the climate units keep theirs. Fields and Storage Units draw nothing and have nothing to switch.
  • Poles only stretch, never lead. Switched-on machines sit together inside their generator's 11 × 11, so most calls need no poles at all. Poles are only called for when the switched-on set spreads past one cluster — and then just a few, never the whole count in Step 1. Extra generators and poles stay in storage.
  • Headroom is the point. A grid loaded near its cap runs at plain speed; a grid with supply to spare runs faster, up to 120%. The call keeps the spare power on purpose instead of switching everything on and slowing the lot.

    Step 5

    🛠️ Add & Adjust

    ⭐ Best plants the full field your RV allows. 🪶 Minimum plants one plot of each crop.
    Plots needing the same climate building share its zones, so only the buildings the plots fill go out — never more than your Step 1 counts. Sizes: Farmland plot 2 × 2 tiles, Woodland plot 3.5 × 3.5 tiles, one climate building covers a 9 × 9 square.
    Center rule, and no overlaps: a plot counts as covered when its center is inside the climate square — a plot parked at the edge with only a sliver inside does not count, though a plot whose center is inside can still hang over the zone's edge and count in full — and the climate building takes up space too. The building stands in the middle of its zone on its own tiles, so no plot is ever placed on them and no two plots overlap each other — plots pack around the building, largest plots first: one zone holds up to 24 Farmland or 8 Woodland plots on their own (24 Farmland around a Cooling Unit too), and when the two lands share a zone the Woodland plots go in first and the Farmland plots fill the space around them, so a shared zone holds far more than 8 in all — 1 Woodland with 18 Farmland fits under a single building. The plan always puts out the fewest buildings that cover its plots: if everything needing a Sunlamp fits under one, one goes out, however many you own. Keep edge plots clear of other climate buildings, so nothing blends by accident.

    🗺️ Suggested Homeland Layout

    This is a suggestion only. Follow it, change it, or ignore it: it never changes anything else in the planner. It shows one tidy way any player can arrange a homeland. A layout is mostly about the farm, so this one is built around the farm and trees: what to grow, and what to have cooking for orders. The Farmlands and Woodlands all sit together in one farm block because they are what people move most, kept together so they are easy to swap and rearrange. When an unlocked plot would be left open anyway, the plot directly beside the farm block is the one left open as moving room, and the later groups start after that open plot, so swapping a Farmland or a Woodland never means working around the other buildings. Like goes with like, with farms and trees together, the cooking buildings on the plots right beside the farm so what is grown and what gets cooked for orders stays together, then the other workshops, Mines together, leisure pieces together, Hatchinators together and the wet works together, and Storage Units spread through the whole layout — never clustered in one spot — each one beside the pieces it serves, because hauling is zone-local: the less an Aniimo walks to storage, the faster things get done. Placement follows abilities and zone coverage, not just kind: crops are laid onto the board in climate order, so the crops that share a climate sit together inside one unit's reach, and the unit lines under the board are worked out per climate building from the crops actually planted; the machines the power call switches to E-Mode go down together first, so one generator's 11 by 11 reaches the whole cluster; and the rest of the machines are grouped by the ability that runs them, so the machines one Aniimo covers between them sit side by side. The farm plots are packed into one tight block, side by side and stacked so their corners meet, and that is for climate efficiency: a climate unit covers a 9 by 9 area around itself and a farm plot counts as covered only when its center falls inside that area (the center rule), so one unit dropped at a corner shared by farm plots can cover all of them at once, because their centers sit inside its 9 by 9. It is worked out fresh from your RV level and the facility counts in Step 1 every time it draws, on the true homeland grid: 16 plots in a 4 by 4 arrangement, each plot 20 big squares across by 15 up, so the whole board is 80 across by 60 up. Plot numbers are the unlock order, so at RV level N you have plots 1 to N. Groups take the earliest plots you have unlocked, in number order, and stay compact, apart from that one moving room plot beside the farm block when a plot is spare: at a low RV or with small counts they use fewer plots, and any unlocked plot no group needs is left open. Locked plots show muted with their number. The board redraws itself whenever anything anywhere in the planner changes — your RV level, a Step 1 count, your basket, your strategy or goals, an Always pin, a copy added or taken back in Step 5, a crop added or removed, even one copy switched off in Step 5 — and it is rebuilt from the plan as it stands at that moment, never from an older one. Facilities are still all placed, even the ones your current plan is not using: a piece no recipe, strategy, pin or added copy is running — or a copy you set to Off in Step 5, or a farm spot with no crop assigned — stays on the board in its place, faded with a dashed outline and marked OFF, so you can still see where it would go. The moment a change makes a plan use it, it switches back on by itself on the redraw; taking a facility off in Step 1 is the only thing that removes it from the board. Hover or tap any Farmland or Woodland piece and it says which crop or tree that spot should grow, from your current plan — the same planting the growing list after Step 4 lays out.
